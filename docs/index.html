The idea

CLIP knows what is in an image but often not how it fits together. Most fixes train it to reject edited "hard negative" captions. GPS-CLIP does the opposite: it pulls the image toward its own grounded structure, its regions, their descriptions, and the relations between them.

Scene graphs are used only while fine-tuning. The model you deploy is a plain CLIP dual encoder with the same architecture, the same inputs, and no extra inference cost.

Three losses, one embedding space

An image and its caption are decomposed into regions and a scene graph. Three complementary objectives then act on the same image and text encoders.

Global

The standard image–caption contrastive loss, ℒglobal. Kept as is, so zero-shot ability is preserved.

Region

Image sub-regions are matched to their text spans, ℒregion. Overlapping regions count as weighted positives instead of being pushed apart.

Relation

Each image is aligned with its ⟨s, r, o⟩ triplets, ℒrelation. Order matters: "dog chasing frisbee" is not "frisbee chasing dog".

Overlap is not a mistake

Regions cut from the same image often overlap. A standard contrastive loss would treat every other region as a negative, punishing the model for noticing that two boxes show the same thing. GPS-CLIP decides by overlap instead.

Results

Fine-tuned from CLIP ViT-B/32, GPS-CLIP improves every benchmark we report, including zero-shot ImageNet and retrieval, which hard-negative methods often trade away.

Why it's practical

  • Nothing new at inferenceSame dual encoder, no added parameters, latency or FLOPs.
  • Training-time structure onlyScene graphs come from an offline multimodal model and are dropped after fine-tuning.
  • Tolerates noisy graphsPerformance degrades gracefully when region spans and triplets are corrupted.
  • Loss- and scale-agnosticWorks with InfoNCE and SigLIP-style sigmoid losses, and scales to ViT-L/14.
